1Your 3 decisions — now answered by evidence

These were guesses an hour ago. They aren't anymore.

Fork A — How do auto-fixes reach a client's live site?

→ Edge-served from our platform + CMS push. NOT a client-side script.
Hard finding: AI crawlers (GPTBot, ClaudeBot, PerplexityBot) do not run JavaScript — 69% of them see only raw HTML (searchviu). So a GTM/JS snippet that injects schema is invisible to the exact engines we're optimizing for. Winner: serve llms.txt, robots.txt and JSON-LD from our edge (stable URL, the client adds one CNAME/redirect — works on any stack, no code access), with a WordPress-API push for in-page schema. Proven at scale by Scrunch on Akamai (scrunch) and Cloudflare's own edge-SEO workers (cloudflare).

Fork B — Build now, or plan first?

→ Phased build. Ship the near-term win now; green-light Phase 1 of the platform on approval.
Reuse everything: the geo skills become the engine (wrapped in a tiny FastAPI), n8n runs the fixes, the Flask app stays as your internal cockpit until the Next.js client app reaches parity. 12-week path, 4 phases — nothing thrown away.

Fork C — Where does the data live?

→ New dedicated Supabase project (not Mission Control's).
This holds paying clients' data + logins + their site credentials. It needs its own row-level-security boundary, billing and isolation — and could be spun out as its own asset later. Mixing it with internal ops data is the one thing not to do.
